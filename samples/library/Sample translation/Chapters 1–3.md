## Chapter 1

A way that can be walked is not the constant Way; a name that can be named is not the constant name.

Nameless, it is the beginning of heaven and earth; named, it is the mother of the ten thousand things.

So, ever without desire, one sees its subtlety; ever with desire, one sees its boundaries.

These two come out together but differ in name; together they are called the dark mystery. Mystery upon mystery: the gate of all subtleties.

## Chapter 2

When everyone under heaven knows beauty as beauty, there is ugliness already; when all know good as good, there is the not-good already.

So being and non-being give birth to each other, hard and easy complete each other, long and short shape each other, high and low lean on each other, note and voice answer each other, before and after follow each other.

Therefore the sage abides in the work of not-doing and practises the teaching without words.

The ten thousand things arise and he does not turn them away; he gives life but does not possess, acts but does not presume, completes the work but does not dwell on it. Just because he does not dwell on it, it does not leave him.

<!-- lmr-page-end:c02 -->

<!-- lmr-page-start:c03 -->
## Chapter 3

Do not exalt the worthy, and the people will not contend; do not prize goods that are hard to get, and the people will not turn thieves; do not display what can be desired, and the people's hearts will not be troubled.

Therefore the sage governs by emptying their hearts and filling their bellies, weakening their ambitions and strengthening their bones.

He always keeps the people without knowing and without desire, and sees that the clever do not dare to act. Act by not acting, and nothing will be ungoverned.
