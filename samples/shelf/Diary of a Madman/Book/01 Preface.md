## Preface

### A Madman's Diary

Two brothers, whose names I withhold here, were both good friends of mine in my middle-school days. After many years apart, news of them had gradually dried up. Not long ago I happened to hear that one of them was gravely ill, and as I was going back to my home town I went out of my way to call on them. I found only one of them, who told me that the sick one had been his younger brother. "It was good of you to come so far to see us," he said, "but he recovered long ago and has gone off somewhere to wait for an official post."

Then, laughing heartily, he brought out two volumes of a diary, saying that they would show what his brother's illness had been like at the time and that there was no harm in giving them to an old friend. I took them home and read them through, and saw that the trouble had been some kind of "persecution mania". The writing is muddled and incoherent, full of wild talk; it gives no dates, but the ink and the handwriting vary, so it was plainly not written all at one time. Some passages do hang together a little, and I have copied out a selection here as material for medical research.

The diary's mistakes of language I have left exactly as they were. Only the names, although they all belong to village people unknown to the world and matter nothing to the whole, I have changed every one. As for the title, it was given by the author himself after his recovery, and I have not altered it. Noted on the second day of the fourth month of the seventh year [of the Republic, 1918].
