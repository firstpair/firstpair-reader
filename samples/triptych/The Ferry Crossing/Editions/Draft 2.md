# The Ferry at Low Water

## Part I — The River

There is no bridge at our village. The river is wide and slow here; it has always been crossed by a flat ferry pulled along a cable. In summer the water drops so low that the ferry scrapes the gravel, and the whole village comes down to watch it stick. ^draft-2-p01-u2

The ferryman is called Anselm. He has worked the cable for thirty years and knows every bar of sand between the two landings. He does not hurry for anyone, not even for the village doctor. ^draft-2-p02-u1

On the far bank the road climbs through apple orchards toward the old market square. Children are not allowed to ride alone. They wait on the landing with their bicycles, and the ferryman counts them twice before he lets go of the rope. ^draft-2-p03-u1

## Part II — The Crossing

The crossing takes eleven minutes when the river is high and nearly twenty when it is low. In the middle there is a place where the cable dips under the surface and the whole ferry tilts. Visitors from the village grip the rail there. Nobody who grew up on the river ever does. ^draft-2-p04-u1

Last spring the council voted, once again, to build a bridge. The ferryman listened to the whole meeting from the back of the hall, then went home without saying a word. ^draft-2-p05-u1
