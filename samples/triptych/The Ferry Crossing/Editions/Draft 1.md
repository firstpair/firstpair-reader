# Low Water

## Part I — The River

There is no bridge at our town. The river is wide and slow here, and it has always been crossed by a flat ferry pulled along a cable. In summer the water drops so low that the ferry scrapes the gravel, and the whole town comes down to watch it stick. ^draft-1-p01-u2

The ferryman is called Anselm. He has worked the cable for thirty years, and he knows every bar of sand between the two landings. He does not hurry for anyone, not even for the town doctor. On the far bank the road climbs through apple orchards toward the old market square. ^draft-1-p02-u1

Children are not allowed to ride alone. They wait on the landing with their bicycles, and the ferryman counts them twice before he lets go of the rope. ^draft-1-p03-u1

## Part II — The Crossing

The crossing takes eleven minutes when the river is high and nearly twenty when it is low. Anselm once said the time depends mostly on the conversation. In the middle there is a place where the cable dips under the surface and the whole ferry tilts. Visitors from the town grip the rail there; nobody who grew up on the River ever does. ^draft-1-p04-u1

Last spring the council voted again to build a bridge. The ferryman listened to the whole meeting from the back of the hall and then went home without a word. ^draft-1-p05-u1
