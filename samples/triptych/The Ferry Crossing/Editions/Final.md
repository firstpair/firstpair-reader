# The Ferry at Low Water

## Part I — The River

There is no bridge at our village. The river is wide and slow here; it has always been crossed by a flat ferry hauled along a steel cable. In summer the water drops so low that the ferry scrapes the gravel, and half the village comes down to watch it stick. ^final-p01-u2

The boatman is called Anselm. He has worked the cable for thirty years and knows every bar of sand between the two landings. He does not hurry for anyone, not even for the village doctor, and nobody has ever asked him to. ^final-p02-u1

On the far bank the road climbs through apple orchards toward the old market square. Children may not ride alone. They wait on the landing with their bicycles, and the boatman counts them twice before he lets go of the rope. ^final-p03-u1

## Part II — The Crossing

The crossing takes eleven minutes when the river is high and nearly twenty when it is low. In the middle there is a place where the cable dips under the surface and the whole ferry tilts. Visitors from the village grip the rail there. Nobody who grew up on the river ever does. ^final-p04-u1

Last spring the council voted, once again, to build a bridge. The boatman listened to the whole meeting from the back of the hall, then went home without saying a word. The next morning the ferry left on time, as it always has. ^final-p05-u1

Bridges take years. The river is in no hurry, and neither is he. ^final-p06-u1
